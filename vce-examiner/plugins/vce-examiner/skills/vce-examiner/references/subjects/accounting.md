# VCE Accounting (Study Design from 2025)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications (2025), 2025 external assessment report, 2026 NHT report. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 100 marks, all compulsory: practical recording and reporting plus theory/analysis.
- The study design's "Characteristics of the study" section (pp. 11–16: qualitative characteristics, assumptions, report formats) is examinable.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Format matters**: correct titles, General Journal entries **with narrations** (naming the source document and giving explanatory detail), properly balanced General Ledger accounts, correct report headings and sections.
- **Respect the constraints in the question** (e.g. an internal control *other than* the cameras and bag checks already in use).
- **Describe/explain/analyse** means more than naming. Give consequences (at least two when asked) and *how* they affect the business.
- Theory answers need the **qualitative characteristic or assumption applied** to the scenario (e.g. verifiability = source documents as evidence).
- Discussions with recommendations must weigh **financial *and* ethical** considerations and end with a clear recommendation. Repeating the question's data is not discussion.

## Recurring technical errors
- **Source documents**: classify the transaction correctly (a sales return isn't a sale or a purchase return). Use the markup to get the cost price. Reverse inventory and cost of sales correctly.
- **GST**: calculate on the amount actually paid. Don't treat GST-exclusive amounts as inclusive. Record GST in sales and sales returns. GST collected isn't operating cash for a credit-only business in that way.
- **Ledgers**: post debits and credits on the correct sides. Opening balances vs transactions. No GST in prepaid expense accounts. Balance day adjustments (prepaid, accrued, doubtful debts allowance).
- **Inventory**: product costs (freight in, etc., included in inventory cost) vs period costs. A delivery charge isn't a separate expense when it's a product cost. Write-downs to **net realisable value** for all affected items. FIFO is a *cost assignment assumption*, not physical movement.
- **Cash Flow Statement**: cash items only. No discount expense, no non-cash items. Correct sections and titles. Know why net cash from operations can exceed net profit (timing, non-cash expenses, collections from receivables).
- **Budgeting**: budgets can be prepared at a different frequency from actual reports. Model changes accurately (apply GP% and wage rates).
- **Indicators**: Return on Assets is based on **net profit** (not revenue). Know the relationships between Asset Turnover, Net Profit Margin and ROA. Inventory turnover and accounts payable turnover trends: describe the consequences (cash flow, discounts lost, supplier relationships).
- **Non-financial indicators** (e.g. number of credit notes → customer satisfaction) must be explained as non-financial, not as dollar values.
