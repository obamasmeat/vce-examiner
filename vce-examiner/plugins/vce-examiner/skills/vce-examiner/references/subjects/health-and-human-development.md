# VCE Health and Human Development (Study Design from 2025)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 50% of study score. 90 marks.
- Section A (80): short and extended answer, with stimulus (case studies, data, media).
- Section B (10): one extended response, marked holistically against three criteria (paraphrased): explain and apply health / human development concepts; examine concepts across **more than one area of study**; **synthesise, analyse and interpret multiple stimulus pieces** to draw conclusions.

## The 10-mark extended response
- Structure it around **the key points of the question**, not source by source (going Source 1 → 2 → 3 produces disjointed restatement).
- Integrate the stimulus with your own knowledge. Without stimulus references, the top marks are out of reach. **Signpost sources** with quotation marks and "(Source 3)".
- *Analyse* implications, don't just state them, then link to **health outcomes** (not human development, unless that's asked).

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Be specific, with direction**: not "impacts life expectancy" but "**decreases** life expectancy". Not "increases morbidity" but "increases morbidity from lung cancer". Give a specific nutrient (vitamin C, fibre, potassium), not "vitamins". Spell out what "belonging" is to (community, culture).
- **Use different examples/outcomes** for each part when two are asked for.
- **Both populations** must be referred to when describing a variation. Use data **with units** (%).
- **Trend** = change over a period, not a single point in time.
- **Comparisons** must compare. Similarities can say "both".
- Answer the exact focus: *preventing* obesity or violence (not the effects of obesity or violence). *Why* food and income are prerequisites (not what happens without them). The *implications* of mass migration (not its causes). Exclusions like "other than body weight" or "other than education and income".
- Don't abbreviate "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander" (it's considered culturally inappropriate).

## Concept precision expected
- **Health and wellbeing**: dynamic = changing. Dimensions (physical, social, mental, emotional, spiritual) with specific examples.
- **Health status indicators**: life expectancy, HALE, DALY (YLL + YLD), morbidity, mortality, incidence, prevalence, self-assessed health. Name them exactly.
- **Biological factors**: high blood pressure (not "hypertension") is the factor. Body weight is excluded where stated.
- **Healthy eating guides**: the AGHE and the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Guide show *proportions* of food groups, not amounts.
- **Social model of health** (addresses broader determinants, empowers, intersectoral, targets whole communities, access). **Ottawa Charter action areas** must actually be evident in the case study (e.g. don't claim "Build Healthy Public Policy" if it isn't there), with a link to how it improves the specific behaviour.
- **Medicare/PBS**: **equity ≠ equality**. Equity = features addressing disadvantage (safety net, concession pricing). Funding sources. Link to health outcomes.
- **Sustainability** (social, economic, environmental) always includes "now and into the future".
- **Social justice** principles (equal opportunity, fair distribution of resources, participation).
- **WHO** objectives vs the goal ("promote, provide, protect" belongs to the goal, not the objectives). Use key terms from the 2025 WHO document.
- **Human Development Index**: indicators (life expectancy *at birth*, mean/expected years of schooling, GNI *per capita*) vs dimensions. Don't confuse the two.
- **Aid**: bilateral = one country's government to another country's government. Emergency aid = in times of crisis or disaster. Multilateral = through organisations. Features of effective aid linked to characteristics of human development.
- **SDGs**: know the key features (SDG 4 covers boys *and* girls). Show how achieving one SDG contributes to another (e.g. SDG 6 → SDG 3).
- Low- vs high-income country characteristics must be *causes*. "Low access to water" can't explain "low access to water".
