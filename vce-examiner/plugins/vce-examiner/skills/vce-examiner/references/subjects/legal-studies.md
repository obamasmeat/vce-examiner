# VCE Legal Studies (Study Design from 2024)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 50% of study score. 80 marks.
- Section A (40): short and extended answer, including one **10-mark** extended response.
- Section B (40): scenario-based short and extended answer, with at least two scenarios (actual, hypothetical or both) and multiple sources.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Command terms decide the ceiling.** *Evaluate* = strengths **and** weaknesses **plus an overall judgement** (no judgement = no full marks). *Discuss* = many-sided (strengths, limitations, circumstances). *Analyse* = significance, relationships, cause and effect, impact. *To what extent* = a clear contention about degree. *Explain* = why/how, with supporting detail.
- **Limiting words**: "one factor" means one only. Respect word limits. Exclusions like "other than the referendum process" must be followed.
- **Use the scenario/stimulus properly**: in Section B, link points to the named parties and facts, and use *several* sources and the connections between them, not just names dropped in.
- **No rote definitions** in separate paragraphs. Weave definitions in only when needed.
- **Use cases, legislation and data** studied during the year to back up Section A points.
- Answer the *focus*: e.g. the appropriateness of a class action in *this* case, not what class actions are. The *impact on Commonwealth law-making power*, not a retelling of the 1967 referendum. The *reason* for reform (why), not the change (what).
- "Similarities and differences" doesn't need equal numbers, but it must refer to the scenario.
- Evaluations of methods must evaluate the **method** (e.g. judicial determination), not the courts as an institution ("courts have delays" drifts off-task).
- 10-mark questions are marked holistically: 2–4 well-developed points can be enough if they're explored in depth.

## Content precision assessors expected
- **Burden and standard of proof**: who carries it (plaintiff/prosecution), balance of probabilities vs beyond reasonable doubt, applied to the parties named. Go beyond the basic contrast (e.g. disclosure obligations).
- **Constitution as a check**: express rights (s 80 jury for indictable Commonwealth offences, s 51(xxxi) just terms, s 92 free trade, s 116 religion, s 117 state-of-residence discrimination). Note how narrow they are. Role of the High Court and **standing**. Division of powers, bicameral structure, separation of powers.
- **Referendum process**: a Constitutional Alteration Bill passed by an **absolute majority** of both houses (or one house twice, three months apart), then a double majority. Factors for success: bipartisan support, clarity of the proposal, public education. 1967: amending s 51(xxvi) and removing s 127 made a residual area **concurrent** (it is not exclusive) → Commonwealth laws, s 109 override.
- **s 109**: the Commonwealth law prevails, and the state law is invalid *to the extent of the inconsistency*. Describe the practical effect.
- **Courts and parliament**: codification and abrogation of common law. Statutory interpretation. Superior courts can change precedent, and lower courts then follow the new precedent.
- **Criminal justice**: plea negotiations (purpose: certainty, reduced charges, saves time and cost). Right to silence ↔ presumption of innocence (no adverse inference, onus on the prosecution). Sentencing factors: victim impact statements, aggravating/mitigating factors. Sanctions linked to their purposes.
- **Civil justice**: jurisdiction (County Court can't hear class actions, so Supreme or Federal Court). Remedies (damages, restoring the plaintiff). Dispute resolution methods (mediation, conciliation, arbitration, judicial determination, VCAT) evaluated against **fairness, equality and access**. Don't confuse CLCs with VLA or CAV.
- **Law reform**: media (informing, gauging support, influencing opinion, across media types). Parliamentary committees vs Royal Commissions vs the VLRC (don't confuse them). Factors affecting parliament: bicameral structure / hostile upper house, community views, international pressures.
