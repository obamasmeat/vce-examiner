# VCE English Language (Study Design 2024–2028)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, criteria and expected qualities, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 75 marks.
- **Section A (15, 20%):** short answers on one or more spoken/written texts.
- **Section B (30, 40%):** an analytical commentary on the language of a text.
- **Section C (30, 40%):** one essay from three, with stimulus material.
- Texts can be transcripts, interviews, social media, ads, speeches, academic or technical writing, and more.

## Criteria (paraphrased)
Use metalanguage to describe and analyse language structures, features and functions. Analyse features of written and spoken English across registers. Analyse language and identity. Analyse attitudes to varieties of Australian English. Draw on **contemporary discussions and debates**. Write clearly organised, controlled responses.

**Section B top band:** confident, detailed analysis of a range of features. Excellent understanding of function, purpose, tenor, register, audience and the influence of **situational and cultural context**. Highly relevant examples with **line references**. Precise metalanguage. A tightly structured commentary.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Metalanguage must be from the current study design.** Terms changed or were added in 2024. Using non-study-design labels, or mislabelling (e.g. a modal verb), loses marks.
- **Quote only what's needed** and stay **specific to the text**. Generic, formulaic comments ("this creates a casual tone") with no salient feature are capped. Don't list features or summarise the text.
- Every feature identified must have its **function/effect in context** explained (e.g. information flow → its impact; politeness strategy → the tenor it creates).
- Multi-requirement questions: hit every part (e.g. identify a positive/negative **face** need *and* the strategy *and* its effect on tenor; discuss **both** speakers).
- Situational context = **setting, field, tenor, mode, audience**. Apply Jakobson's functions *to this text* and say when or why the function shifts.
- **Identity**: link features (turn-taking, lexis, register shifts) to individual and group identity.
- Briefly plan multi-mark answers so the integrated examples have a clear direction.

## Section C essay
- **Unpack every key word** in the stimulus proposition *and* the question (e.g. "register", "politeness", "social standing"). This was the strongest marker of high scorers.
- Sustained expository argument across Units 3–4 knowledge, with precise metalanguage.
- **Contemporary, specific examples** (recent media, public debates, Australian English varieties, Aboriginal Englishes, slang, PC language, euphemism) with dates or sources. Engage the stimulus but go beyond it.
- Explore what the concepts actually mean (e.g. what it means for language to "reflect" identity) rather than assuming it.
