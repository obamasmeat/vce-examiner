# VCE Environmental Science (Study Design from 2022)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 50% of study score. 120 marks.
- Section A: 30 MC (30). Section B: short and extended answer on scenarios, case studies and experiments (90).
- Key science skills and the study design's terminology (pp. 16–19) are examinable.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Copying the stem isn't an answer** (e.g. "they are traditional custodians" copied out as the reason for cultural value).
- **Use the data**: explanations without figures from the table/graph lose marks. Definitions alone don't score.
- Make the key point clearly and **don't contradict yourself**. Know the command terms (state / explain / justify).
- Show working in calculations (percentage change, energy efficiency, Simpson's Index, costs over a lifespan). Basic maths is examinable.
- Answer the *specific* focus (e.g. why *genetic diversity* data was collected, not why population data matters in general). Costs and benefits of the **change** in land use, not of the original use.
- Read exclusions in the stem ("other than CO₂ and water vapour").

## Terminology traps (study design terms are tested precisely)
- **Precautionary principle**: applies when there's a *lack of full scientific certainty*. A ban based on established evidence is not precaution.
- **Mitigation** (reducing causes/emissions) vs **adaptation** (helping systems adjust to effects).
- **Bioaccumulation** (build-up within an organism) vs **biomagnification** (increase up the food chain).
- **Intergenerational** vs **intragenerational** equity. Efficiency of resource use.
- **Validity** isn't accuracy, precision or "no errors": it's whether the investigation measures what it intends to.
- Energy classifications: nuclear = non-fossil, non-renewable. Biomass = renewable, non-fossil. Coal stores chemical (potential) energy.
- Ecosystem services categories (cultural services = non-material benefits: sense of place, identity).
- Mechanical vs biological rehabilitation strategies.
- Units: turbidity in NTU.

## Skills and content
- **Simpson's Index of Diversity** = 1 − D (don't forget to subtract). Then *interpret* it (closer to 1 = more diverse) and compare the sites. Relative abundance ≠ raw count.
- **Mark-recapture**: capture, mark, release, recapture, then use the ratio to estimate the population.
- Grids vs transects vs quadrats: why one suits *this* study. Both work for flora and fauna.
- Baseline data: its purpose is to monitor change and judge whether a program is working.
- Small population → low genetic diversity → less chance of resistance to new disease.
- **Greenhouse effect**: absorption and re-emission of infrared by gases, plus albedo. Explain the mechanism, not just "traps heat". Pre-industrial fluctuations come from natural causes (climate cycles, volcanic activity).
- IPCC confidence ratings: explain what the confidence level means about evidence and agreement.
- Energy decisions: account for intermittency (storage/backup) when justifying wind or solar. Compare lifetime costs including replacements.
- Investigation aims must match the data that was actually collected.
- Link climate model findings to a **specific** risk for the named industry.
