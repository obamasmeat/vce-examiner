# VCE Physics (Study Design from 2024)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 h 30 min writing. 50% of study score. 120 marks.
- Section A: 20 MC (20). Section B: short and extended answer (100).
- Key science skills are examinable. One outcome is experimental design, and it is examined heavily. A formula sheet is provided, and students bring an A3 sheet of notes.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **"Show that" needs a complete substitution** with values in their units. A formula alone is not working. Never fudge the last line to match the given answer: assessors notice.
- **Box with a unit = answer in that unit** (e.g. kW). Watch the significant figures asked for.
- **Option + explain**: choosing an option without an attempted justification earns nothing.
- **Generic statements score zero.** Explanations must refer to the specific objects in the question (this magnet, this solenoid, this electron).
- **Tautologies earn nothing**: restating the stem as the reason (e.g. "transformers need AC because they need alternating current in the primary").
- **Use the method the stem specifies** (e.g. "using the gradient from part f"). Substituting table data instead gets no marks.
- Write in pen. Faint pencil on scanned scripts may be misread.

## Graph skills (lost marks every year)
- **Line of best fit** must reflect all points. Don't rule it through the first and last points only.
- **Gradient**: show rise/run with two points **on the line**, far apart, read accurately (watch ×10⁻⁶ axis multipliers). Table data points not on the line give a wrong gradient.
- Scale graphs to use the available space.
- Planck's constant from the gradient: *demonstrate* the gradient calculation. Don't just quote h.

## Recurring physics errors
- **Unit conversions**: km → m (and add Earth's radius to altitude for orbits), cm → m, ms → s, mm/nm → m, GV → V. Squaring distances correctly.
- **Sign conventions**: keep g consistently directed in projectile problems. Don't apply the range equation where launch and landing heights differ. Pre-memorised formulas from an A3 sheet misapplied.
- **Forces**: at constant velocity there's no net force, so don't draw an "Fnet" arrow. sin vs cos/tan on inclines. Include friction, and use the correct mass (not the total of every object). On banked tracks the normal force does *not* point to the centre.
- **Impulse/momentum**: the area under a force–time graph is impulse, but under a force–*displacement* graph it's work. Get direction of change in momentum right. Inelastic = **kinetic** energy not conserved. Momentum *is* conserved, so don't say otherwise.
- **Airbags**: the justification must compare the two collisions (longer time → smaller force for the same impulse), not just quote the formula.
- **Fields**: know electric fields as well as gravitational and magnetic ones. Negative charge reverses the force direction from the right-hand rule. No force on a wire when the current is parallel to the field. Distinguish what changes (time, acceleration) from what doesn't (final kinetic energy).
- **Induction**: explain using changing *flux* through the solenoid. Opposite polarities on entry and exit. "The magnet has a changing field" isn't enough. Reciting Lenz's law isn't an explanation.
- **Motors/generators**: name the **split-ring commutator** vs **slip rings** precisely and explain their function *in the device asked about*. Don't confuse motors with generators.
- **Transmission**: step-up reduces line current, and I²R loss falls. Step-down returns usable voltage (not "to reduce current to a safe level"). RMS vs peak confusions. Parallel circuits from Units 1–2 are assumed knowledge.
- **Relativity**: the postulate is the speed of light being constant **for all observers regardless of relative motion**. Michelson–Morley: two **perpendicular** paths, null result. Keep frames of reference straight (in its own frame the muon doesn't "experience time dilation"). Don't mix time-dilation and length-contraction arguments. γ ≥ 1 always.
- **Light/quantum**: diffraction (ratio of wavelength to gap) vs interference (slit separation) are different. Diffraction is still observable when λ/w < 1. Photoelectric effect: talk about photo*electrons* and **maximum** kinetic energy. A different frequency changes the intercept, not the gradient. At equal power, shorter wavelength means fewer photons and a lower photocurrent. Use the correct Planck's constant (eV vs J). Emission vs absorption arrows.
- **Experimental design**: identify the independent, dependent and controlled variables explicitly. Repeated measurements reduce the effect of **random** error (study design wording). Systematic error is the same each repeat, random error varies.
- Multi-step application problems: plan a strategy first (what will you calculate and compare it to?). Many students left these blank.
