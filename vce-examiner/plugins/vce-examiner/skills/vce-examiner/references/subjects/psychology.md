# VCE Psychology (Study Design 2023–2027)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications (2025), 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 h 30 min writing. 50% of study score. 120 marks.
- Section A: 40 MC (40). Section B: short and extended answer (80), including **one 10-mark extended response with no parts**.
- Stimulus material: scenarios, data, media texts, investigations. Key science skills are examinable.

## The 10-mark extended response
Marked **holistically** against four criteria (paraphrased):
1. Identifying and explaining the relevant psychological **terminology** in a new context.
2. **Analysing and discussing** the relevant concepts and how they connect.
3. **Analysing/evaluating** the data, methods, models or theories in the question.
4. Building **evidence-based arguments**, conclusions or implications.

What high scorers did:
- Defined *and* used every term in the question, plus extra relevant terms (e.g. encoding, retrieval cue, hippocampus, LTP/LTD, pruning).
- Addressed **every part** of the question. Subheadings and full sentences helped.
- **Compare** = at least one detailed similarity *and* one detailed difference.
- **Used the stimulus explicitly**: quoted or described diagram features and linked them ("The diagram shows… This demonstrates…"). Just writing "as shown in the diagram" isn't enough.
- **Evaluate** = a justified judgement for *each* thing evaluated, built on "because" reasoning, with strengths **and** limitations.
- Used precise wording: "mnemonic device", not "memory trick". Aphantasia = difficulty forming mental images, not an inability to imagine.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Definitions alone don't score.** Apply them to the scenario. Generic, non-contextual answers are capped (e.g. 4/5 at most when the scenario is ignored).
- **Only the first answer is marked** when one is asked for.
- "Two differences" must be **congruent**: compare the *same feature* for both things (e.g. speed of action for neurotransmitters vs neuromodulators).
- Answer the question asked (why a correlational study is *more ethical*, not the ethical problems of experiments). Give a suggestion when one is asked for.
- "Summarise" = a concise account of the major details. Know the VCAA command-term glossary.

## Content traps assessors flagged
- **Nervous system**: sweating and thermoregulation are autonomic and brain-mediated, not spinal reflexes. Neurons don't "travel"; neural messages do. Conscious vs unconscious examples must fit the scenario.
- **Learning**: classical conditioning needs the NS presented immediately **before** the UCS, with the shortest effective delay. Identify NS/UCS/UCR/CS/CR precisely (the *sound* of the bell). Distinguish operant from classical and from observational learning. Negative reinforcement = removal of an unpleasant stimulus (praise is positive reinforcement).
- **Memory**: know the roles of the basal ganglia, neocortex and hippocampus, and how they interact. Link Atkinson-Shiffrin stores to the actual stimulus, not just describe the model. Episodic vs semantic memory in imagined futures. Alzheimer's: a biological change + the brain region + the impact on retrieval.
- **LTP/LTD**: LTD isn't "no stimulation". Explain at the neural level (sprouting, pruning, strengthened/weakened pathways).
- **Sleep**: daylight is the main zeitgeber. Sleep hygiene for shift workers is timed to *their* sleep period. Explain the mechanism (why meal timing affects sleep onset). Devices must measure something specific (EEG change between REM/NREM). Model limitations must be about the *model*, not general sleep facts.
- **Stress**: acute vs chronic stress are types of *stress*, not stressors. Resilience = the ability to cope with and manage change and uncertainty.
- **Phobia (4P)**: stigma → shame → avoiding treatment → maintenance. Catastrophic thinking is future-oriented and **maintains/worsens** a phobia rather than causing it. Systematic desensitisation replaces fear with calm as the CR.
- **Wellbeing**: name "support" explicitly as a social protective factor. Cultural continuity and songlines. Kinship's role in ways of knowing: describe *how* it supports SEWB, don't restate the question.
- **Gut–brain axis** vs gut microbiota: answer the one asked about.

## Research methods (heavily examined)
- **Hypothesis**: both levels of the IV, the DV, a clear direction, and the population. Phrase it as a prediction.
- IVs are what the researcher manipulates (e.g. order and time delay), not the stimuli themselves.
- Population = the specific group (people with Alzheimer's **who take antibiotics**).
- Correlational studies don't establish cause and effect. Know each methodology on the study design's list.
- Between-subjects vs within vs mixed designs: state the specific benefit in context.
- Ethics: deception is allowed when necessary, provided there's a **debrief**. Don't confuse voluntary participation with withdrawal rights. Make a clear yes/no judgement with justification.
- **Precision** = the smallest standard deviation, explicitly compared. Validity-specific strengths/limitations, not generic ones. Reproducibility = change the conditions **and** compare results.
- Graphs: bar graph for categorical data, labelled axes with scale.
