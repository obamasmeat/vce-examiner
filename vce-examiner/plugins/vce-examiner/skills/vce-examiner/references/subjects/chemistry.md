# VCE Chemistry (Study Design from 2024)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 h 30 min writing. 50% of study score. 120 marks.
- Section A: 30 MC (30 marks). Section B: short and extended answer (90 marks).
- All Units 3–4 key knowledge plus the **key science skills** (cross-study specifications) are examinable. Questions can integrate research methods, ethics and new applications. Data Book provided.

## How marks are awarded
Multi-mark questions are marked as separate steps (e.g. mark 1 = correct mole ratio, mark 2 = final value **with units**). Reproduce this: tell the student which step they got.

## Highest-frequency mark losses
- **Units.** This was the most common error in 2025. If the stem doesn't supply the unit, the answer must state it (g, kJ, mol, mol L⁻¹). Units are needed for resolution values too. Don't use "m/z" as a unit.
- **Answering in the wrong quantity**: a concentration given when the stem asked for an amount in mol. % yield given when both atom economy and % yield were asked.
- **Stoichiometry**: missing dilution or reaction ratios. Not identifying the limiting reagent, or not calculating the excess remaining. Unit conversions from the Data Book (m³ → L) missed.
- **Using outdated Data Book values** (e.g. density of water from the previous study design).
- **Equations**: balanced, with states where needed. Use acidic vs basic media as the stem says (don't balance with OH⁻ in acidic conditions). Single arrow for spontaneous redox in a cell. Correct half-equations at the correct electrode. Draw the cell to work out which is the anode and which is the cathode.
- **Names vs formulas**: if the stem asks for names, formulas lose the mark. Correct IUPAC (propan-1-ol). Correct semi-structural representation (not "OHCH₂…" for a terminal OH).
- **Comparisons must be explicit**: "oleic acid has one C=C" doesn't say what stearic acid has. State both sides.
- **Answers in the specified box/location.** Working written inside an answer box can invalidate it. Responses placed elsewhere may not be found.
- **Read the stem fully**: e.g. "at constant temperature" rules out temperature-based answers.

## Concept-specific traps assessors flagged
- **Equilibrium**: "increased pressure shifts forward" earns nothing unless you say *how* pressure is increased (adding an inert gas raises pressure but doesn't shift it). Use Q vs K reasoning. ICE table + equilibrium expression both needed. A *reversible* reaction is defined by being able to proceed in either direction, not by "forward rate = reverse rate".
- **Redox**: an oxidant is reduced. Don't call a metal being plated an oxidant. Fermentation involves both oxidation and reduction. Electroplating needs a constant electrolyte concentration, linked explicitly to anode and cathode processes.
- **Enzymes**: say the *enzyme* denatures, not that the yeast "dies". Link optimum temperature to the stated condition, not "SLC".
- **Calorimetry**: explain why a calibration factor is needed (energy also heats the calorimeter, not just the water). Watch sign and unit handling (J vs kJ).
- **Spectroscopy**: justify structure from peak *count*, *splitting* and *integration*. Assessors did not accept chemical shift alone to justify structural features in some items. Relate the data to the specific molecule. Identify bonds using Data Book labels (e.g. O–H (alcohol)).
- **Chromatography/HPLC**: identify by running a standard under the same conditions and matching retention time (or spiking: the peak grows). Quantify with a calibration curve made on that set-up, not published data.
- **Tests**: the limewater test applies to the CO₂ from reacting a carboxylic acid with a carbonate, not to the acid itself. Recrystallisation doesn't apply to oils.
- **Structure**: benzene vs cyclohexane. A condensation reaction is defined by eliminating a small molecule. Impurities give a lower, broader melting point range.
- **Green chemistry**: name the principle **and** link it to the specific detail (e.g. lower temperature → catalysis → less energy). When asked for a "conflict", state both sides of it.

## Key science skills (shared across the sciences)
- **Random vs systematic error**: scatter above and below the line of best fit = random. Systematic error can't be judged without a reference value. "Human error" is never acceptable: name the specific source (e.g. subjective judgement of the colour change at the endpoint).
- **Resolution** = the smallest change an instrument can detect, given with units.
- Reference the graph or data directly ("the rate was the same for 15 minutes" without citing the graph = no mark).
- Limitations belong in the discussion and must be specific to this method.
