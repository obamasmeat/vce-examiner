# VCE History Units 3–4: Revolutions, Australian History, Ancient History (Study Design from 2022)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, shared History essay criteria and expected qualities, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports for all three studies. My own summary.

## Exam formats
- All three: 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 50% of study score. 70 marks.
- **Section A** is source-based short and extended answer on the chosen investigations (e.g. two of four revolutions, two of four Australian investigations). Question parts climb in order: *outline/identify* → *explain/analyse* → *evaluate* (often 10 marks, with a source).
- **Section B** is one essay chosen from one per revolution / investigation / society. Topics can come from AOS1, AOS2 or both.
  - Revolutions: America, France, Russia, China.
  - Australian History: four investigations.
  - Ancient History: Egypt, Greece, Rome.

## Essay criteria (shared, paraphrased) and bands
1. A **coherent, relevant argument** that addresses the specific demands of the question.
2. **Accurate, appropriate historical knowledge.**
3. Application of **historical thinking concepts** (cause and consequence, continuity and change, significance, perspectives, interpretations, evidence).
4. **Sources as evidence**: a range of primary sources, perspectives and historical interpretations.

Top band: persuasive argument relevant throughout. Extensive, precise knowledge. Sophisticated use of the concepts. A wide range of primary sources and interpretations integrated seamlessly.
Mid band: coherent but sometimes conventional, drifting from the specific demands. General concept use. Some sources.
Low-mid band: **mostly narrative/descriptive**. Limited, descriptive use of sources.
*All four criteria are needed for a high essay. Many essays had no sources at all, which is a big cap.*

## Essay: what separates the top band
- A **clear contention in the student's own voice** answering *to what extent*: agree / disagree / partially agree, stated early.
- **Unpack the key terms** of the prompt ("unconventional reign", "always", "simply", "mostly", "most significant"). Qualifying absolutes is a top-band move (e.g. "always ruthless violence" → a range of responses: violence, the threat of it, compromise).
- **Nuance**: exceptions to the dominant argument, change over time, arguments for and against.
- **Respect the AOS and timeframe.** Many lost marks by spending too long on AOS1 causes when the prompt was about the new regime (AOS2), or by using background from outside the study design's date range.
- Specific detail: names, dates, places, figures, statistics. Using dates well correlated with higher scores.
- **Historians attributed precisely** (to the right historian of the *right* revolution or topic), paraphrasing their *reasoning*, with short quotes. **Fabricated, misattributed or irrelevant quotes** were singled out as a common failing.
- Conventional views are fine. Originality isn't required, but precision and persuasion are.
- No particular structure is required (three body paragraphs is common and fine).

## Section A source questions
- **Outline the ideas of a source** (part a): an overview of *several* ideas, in full sentences, with short integrated quotes. Not a dot-point list of quotes, not multiple quotes for one idea, and **no own knowledge needed** (it wastes time).
- **Identify** (lower order): transcribe source features accurately, use quotation marks, and read the caption/explanatory notes (they often contain answers).
- **Explain/analyse** (medium order): multiple on-topic points combining the **source + own knowledge**. Responses that only rewrite the source are capped. Stay on the named individual or group (e.g. education only, not women's rights).
- **Evaluate** (10 marks): a clear, reasoned judgement with a structured argument. Use the source as a springboard, not the whole answer. Own knowledge is essential (over-reliance on the source caps the mark).
- Reference sources explicitly: "(Source 11)". Historians' views in Section A count as own knowledge but aren't required.
- Ancient: at least three distinct source points, each embedded in a sentence showing what it means for the question.

## Style warnings (Ancient report, apply to all)
- Don't use Egyptian/Greek/Latin terms without explaining them.
- No "inevitable" or "obvious", and no counterfactuals ("if X hadn't happened…").
- Don't treat one source as representative of a whole career or period.
- Handwriting must be legible: assessors can't mark what they can't read.
- Watch confusions of names and acts (e.g. Coercive vs Revenue Acts, Jiang Qing vs Lin Biao).
