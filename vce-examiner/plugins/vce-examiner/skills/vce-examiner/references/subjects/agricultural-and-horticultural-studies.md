# VCE Agricultural and Horticultural Studies (Study Design from 2020)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 1 h 30 min writing. 40% of study score. 100 marks, all short and extended answer, often stimulus- or scenario-based.
- Prescribed pests, diseases and weeds (study design p. 16) and the named state/federal regulations (Unit 4 Outcome 2) are examinable.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Address every part/bullet** of the question. Scaffolded bullets are each worth marks.
- **Identify + describe/explain the effect.** Naming a technology or strategy alone usually gets 1 mark. Higher marks need *how* it affects a named input or output (yield, efficiency, resource use, cost).
- **Positive and negative** when both are asked for. **Evaluate** = weigh benefits and limitations and give an overall judgement (mid-range answers only described).
- **Analyse** needs depth to match the marks: implications, practicalities, economic/social factors.
- Stick to the count asked for: listing several when one is asked earns nothing extra and wastes time. The same method can't be credited twice.
- Match the scale asked: an **industry-level** response is not a single farm's action, and it must be realistic at industry scale.
- Use the stimulus scenario: fixes for hazards that aren't in the stimulus, or safety for sheep when the question asks about the shearing team, earn no marks.
- Don't just repeat stimulus material. Link it explicitly to sustainability (environmental / economic / social).
- Long answers that far exceed what the mark allocation needs waste time.

## Content traps assessors flagged
- **Regulations/policies**: name the correct Act (the year isn't needed). Two different policies, not one policy applied twice. Many students left this blank, so learn the study design's three named regulations.
- **Weeds**: herbicide (not pesticide or fungicide). Identification must use a feature of *the named weed*. Spread methods explained farm to farm.
- **Soil degradation**: causes must be active (overgrazing, heavy machinery leading to compaction), not "not rotating paddocks" or just "chemicals". Show cause → effect.
- **Pests and diseases**: distinguish **prevention** from **control** strategies (e.g. footbaths and paddock rotation prevent footrot, drenches don't). Explain the mechanism.
- **GMOs**: Bt crops (reduced insecticide) vs Roundup Ready (herbicide tolerance, not necessarily reduced herbicide). Stakeholder views with impact detail.
- **Organic certification** in Australia allows limited, controlled inputs. Don't overstate it.
- **Quality assurance**: explain *how* it's maintained (audits, records, e.g. shearing logs and wool grading), with an example.
- **Marketing**: outline tool or strategy + target market + intended effect. Niche market: identify it, then explain how the strategy persuades.
- **Sustainability**: separate economic, social and environmental, and address all that are asked for.
- **Net zero/carbon footprint**: realistic practices (renewables, livestock management, reduced fertiliser) and the barriers (cost, technology, scale).
