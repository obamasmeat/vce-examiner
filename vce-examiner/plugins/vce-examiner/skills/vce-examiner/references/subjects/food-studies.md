# VCE Food Studies (Study Design from 2023)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 1 h 30 min writing. 90 marks.
- Section A: 20 MC (20). Section B: short and extended answer (70), including one 10-mark question with no parts.
- Practical skills aren't performed, but the knowledge behind them is examinable.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Explicit links** between the scenario and the concept (e.g. living in a shared house → limited kitchen access → reduced food accessibility). Vague statements ("educated people eat better", "good/bad choices") score low.
- **Apply criteria when asked**: assess information against the named criteria (language use, source, ethics, effectiveness, commercial gain). Use **all** the named criteria, refer to the stimulus, and add your own knowledge rather than repeating the stimulus.
- *Discuss* = more than one angle, developed. *Analyse* = elaborate the relationship. *Evaluate* = a judgement.

## Content precision traps
- **Digestion**: the specific roles of the accessory organs (liver/bile emulsification, gall bladder storage, pancreas lipase) in fat digestion, in detail.
- **AGTHE nutritional rationale** is poorly understood: it's a visual guide to the *types and amounts* of foods for health, based on nutrient-dense food groups. Apply it to the food group in the question.
- Life stage needs (e.g. why pregnancy needs more grain serves: energy, fibre, folate, iron) without misconceptions.
- **Wheat allergy ≠ gluten intolerance**: "go gluten-free" doesn't manage a wheat allergy (many wheat proteins aren't gluten, and gluten-free foods may still contain wheat).
- Ethical purchasing: reasons consumers pay more (animal welfare, fair wages, sustainability, local economy), each discussed.
- Over-consumption effects on physical and mental health: specific conditions and mechanisms.
- Media influence on diet trends, with specific examples. Consumer activism actions and their impact on the food system.
- Social factors (education, income, culture, social networks) linked to food choices.
