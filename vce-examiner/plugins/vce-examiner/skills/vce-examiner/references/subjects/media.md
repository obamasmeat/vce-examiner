# VCE Media

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 80 marks.
- Section A (50): short and extended answer from any area of study, often on visual or written stimulus.
- Section B (30): extended answers from Unit 3 AOS1 (narratives) and Unit 4 AOS2 (agency and control).

## Command terms (VCAA defines these, and Media reports stress them)
- **Identify** = name it, briefly. **Outline** = an overview with a bit more detail. **Describe** = accurate characteristics and features. **Discuss** = a balanced, considered argument with points for and against. **Analyse** = key components *and the significance of the relationships between them*, drawing out implications.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Specific examples from the chosen media narrative** (named sequences and scenes), not generalities. For analysis of codes and conventions, show how they **work together** in two specific sequences to convey meaning.
- Representations: link the construction (codes, conventions) to a **specific view or value** of the society it was produced in, and say whether it reflects or challenges it.
- Audience readings: break down the differences between **two contexts** (time, place, culture) and why each reads the narrative differently.
- **Narrative ≠ theme**: in production questions, explain what *happens* (story, structure), not just what it's "about".
- Production design: post-production feedback = name specific methods + how feedback is gathered + how the product is refined + how that engages the intended audience. Skill experiments target one specific technical skill.
- **Agency and control**: one specific way audiences exercise agency, with a **contemporary** example. Media influence claims need evidence from a real, recent case. Explain *how and why* globalised institutions use media.
- **Regulation**: engage with the specific comments or stimulus (e.g. the under-16 social media age restriction), and discuss issues and challenges for both media and audiences with current examples (e.g. regulatory responses to broadcasting breaches).
