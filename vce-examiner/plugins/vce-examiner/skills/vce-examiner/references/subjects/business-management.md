# VCE Business Management (Study Design from 2023)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 50% of study score. 75 marks.
- Section A (40 or 50): short and extended answer. Section B (25 or 35): based on a **case study**.
- One 10-mark extended response (no parts) in either section.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Linking to the case study** is the core skill. Dropping the business name in is *not* a link. A real link uses specific facts (e.g. perishable ingredients, store closures, downsizing) to show *how* the theory applies.
- **Rote, verbatim definitions** earn little. No definition needs set wording; marks reward demonstrated understanding. **Circular definitions** ("recycle is when something is recycled") earn nothing.
- **Difference / compare**: refer explicitly to *both* terms ("retirement is voluntary **whereas** redundancy is involuntary"). Signpost: "A similarity is… A difference is…". Compare = at least one similarity + one difference, explained.
- **Command terms**:
  - *Propose* = state what the strategy would actually involve.
  - *Justify* = give the **benefits** of the choice, not just a description.
  - *Discuss* = strengths and weaknesses.
  - *Evaluate* = pros and cons + an **evaluative judgement**.
  - *Analyse* = reasons + significance, linked to a case study.
  - *Describe* = don't evaluate or give opinions.
- **Read every word**: plurals ("strategies"), trailing clauses ("…to overcome resistance"), the direction of a KPI change. Answer within scope.
- **Efficiency** (resource use) and **effectiveness** (achieving objectives) must be treated separately, never blended. Effectiveness is *not* "how well a product works".
- Write efficiently for 2-mark questions: give only what's required.
- Contemporary case studies are required when asked (e.g. leadership in change), and must be **linked** to the point.

## Theory precision expected (study design terms)
- **Management styles** (autocratic, persuasive, consultative, participative, laissez-faire) and **skills** (decision-making, etc.): identify the style shown in the case and link the skill to it.
- **Motivation theories**: Locke and Latham (clarity, challenge, commitment, feedback, task complexity), explained *and* linked to motivation in context, not just listed. Performance-related pay: strengths and weaknesses.
- **Change**: Lewin's Force Field Analysis (identify driving/restraining forces, **weight** them, rank, implement, evaluate the response). These happen *before* the change. Restraining forces need both *why* and *how* they slow change. Lewin's Three-step model: unfreeze, change, **refreeze = embedding** the change (not evaluating it). Senge's Learning Organisation: personal mastery, mental models, shared vision, team learning, systems thinking, each described and tied to change.
- Low-risk strategies (communication, empowerment, support, incentives) vs high-risk (manipulation, threats). Explain *what kind* of support.
- **Dispute resolution**: mediation is non-binding (parties reach their own agreement), while arbitration is binding. Don't confuse them.
- **Operations**: elements of the operations system (inputs, processes, outputs). Quality (two aspects for 2 marks). TQM features linked to the stated outcome. Waste minimisation (reduce, e.g. JIT / reuse / recycle). AI and CAD for efficiency. Global sourcing vs overseas manufacture vs global outsourcing (non-core functions), kept distinct.
- **CSR**: required across operations, HR and change. Explain *why it matters during change*, not just reputation in general.
- **Employee relations**: retirement vs redundancy vs dismissal. KPIs: why managers track them and what a negative trend signals.
