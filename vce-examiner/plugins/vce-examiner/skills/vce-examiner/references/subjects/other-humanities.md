# Philosophy, Classical Studies, Sociology, Politics, Religion and Society, Texts and Traditions, Geography, Extended Investigation

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports for each study. My own summary.
All of these exams are 15 min reading + 2 hours writing unless noted otherwise.

## Philosophy (60 marks)
- Section A: stimulus-based questions on Unit 3 texts, marked holistically. Section B: one essay from three on Unit 4 (20, holistic).
- **Engage the passage directly**, and say so: answers that never reference "the passage above" aren't complete.
- Pull together every element asked for (e.g. explain the thinker's position *and* connect related concepts: the discipline of suffering ↔ self-discipline).
- Thinkers must come from the **prescribed Unit 3 texts** when specified. Others don't count.
- Notice **shifts of focus** between parts (from self-discipline to suffering). Include an **example** when required.
- Interpret concepts at the scale intended (e.g. justice as political distribution, not only individual charity).

## Classical Studies (80 marks)
- Section A: extracts from set texts. Section B: an essay against criteria.
- Use mark allocation and line count as a guide. Extra plot information wastes time.
- "Outline what occurs immediately before/after": specific events in sequence, not broad summaries.
- Technique questions: label techniques accurately and explain their effect (e.g. pathos). Choose the strongest points. No need to go through chronologically.
- When asked to use the extract **and** the wider book, include both. Avoid narrowing the scope (e.g. only "fate").

## Sociology (80 marks)
- Short and extended answer, with four 10-mark extended questions (one per area of study) marked against criteria.
- Unit 4 AOS1 community: **must not** be an Australian Indigenous community.
- Address misconceptions explicitly (e.g. that all Indigenous Australians share one culture) with examples.
- **Reconciliation**: discuss the *process* and why opinions differ, supported by sourced evidence, not just lists of practical/symbolic examples.
- Material culture = tangible aspects. Belonging: explain how traditions create belonging *for the community itself* (not by educating outsiders).
- Use sourced, specific evidence and relevant case studies, and answer the exact question rather than a prepared case study.

## Politics (Global Politics)
- Three sections from Unit 3 AOS1–2 and Unit 4 (Indo-Pacific focus).
- Causes must be **distinct** and each linked to the issue with specific evidence.
- Name international laws **in full and correctly**.
- Engage with the **specific characteristic** named (e.g. stability *or* change).
- Explain actors' **perspectives → interests → actions**. Don't just narrate events.
- Unit 4 examples must come from the **Indo-Pacific**. Regional standing ≠ regional relationships.
- **Political significance** must be established as the study design defines it.

## Religion and Society (80 marks)
- Section A: short answer (30). Section B: extended answer (50), with at least two 10-mark single-part questions.
- Give the number of ways or points asked for (e.g. two to three, kept clear and distinct).
- Challenges: **precise descriptions** of the tradition's stance(s) and response(s), not a bare "the tradition opposed it". Don't reuse the same challenge where the paper forbids it.
- Significant life experience: beliefs used to interpret it *during and after*. Only the first experience described is marked.
- Sources must be accurate, relevant, cited and not repeated.

## Texts and Traditions
- Answer only the section for your set text (Jeremiah/Ezekiel, Luke, Qur'an, etc.).
- Use the required number of examples from the text and address **all parts** of the question.
- Essays: develop a discussion or explanation using examples from the set text. When asked, name the theme and a later historical period and show how the text was interpreted then.
- Don't recycle answers to past-paper questions.

## Geography (Study Design from 2022, 80 marks)
- Short and extended answer from a **Data Book** (maps, graphs, images).
- **Use data in the answer, with units.** Comparisons must compare (similarities *and* differences).
- Map skills: compass directions precise to 16 points where needed (east vs south-east), distances and lengths with units.
- Use concepts precisely: natural characteristics, glacier vs ice sheet (with examples), human impacts → social conditions → issues and challenges.
- Fieldwork and geospatial skills are examinable.

## Extended Investigation (critical thinking test)
- **Test:** 15 min reading + 1 h 15 min. 50 marks.
- Follow lines of reasoning before classifying statements as for or against.
- Analyse and evaluate opposing findings by sorting evidence and judging its strength and relevance.
- Analyse how data is used (accurately or misleadingly) in commentaries.
- Use reading time to plan. Many responses were incomplete, so manage time across all questions.
