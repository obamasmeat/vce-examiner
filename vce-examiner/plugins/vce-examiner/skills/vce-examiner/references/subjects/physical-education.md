# VCE Physical Education (Study Design from 2025)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 110 marks.
- Section A: 20 MC (20). Section B: short and extended answer (90), including **one 10-mark extended response** on the **interdisciplinary** Unit 4 AOS3 approach (linking knowledge across the course to a stimulus).

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Context is everything**: answers must fit the named sport or athlete (a cool-down for *kayaking*, not "jogging"). Use the stimulus data.
- **Explain via mechanism**: e.g. improved lactate tolerance → **increased muscle buffering capacity** → performance. Respiratory adaptation → which change → VO₂ max → performance. Increased strength → power. Fibre type → speed.
- Answer the timing or context asked: nutrition *during* the event (not carb loading beforehand). Psychological strategies *during competition* (not preventing overtraining).
- **Critique/evaluate** = judge against the correct prescription (e.g. short interval training for speed needs near-maximal intensity, so 7 RPE is too low). Give a clear verdict.
- The 10-mark extended response rewards **explicit interrelationships** between all the listed knowledge points, consistently tied back to the stimulus. Separate mini-essays per dot point score lower.

## Terminology traps flagged
- **Activity analysis** (identifies the physiological requirements of an activity) ≠ **fitness testing** (identifies an athlete's strengths and weaknesses).
- A *type of data* (e.g. movement patterns, skill frequency) ≠ a *collection method* (HR monitor, GPS).
- Practice **distribution** (massed/distributed) ≠ practice **type** (part/whole) ≠ **variability** (blocked/random). Answer the category asked, using stage of learning + skill type.
- **Variety** (a training principle) ≠ **variability** (a practice classification).
- Thermoregulation ≠ thermoregulatory fatigue.
- Coaching: observation → evaluation → error correction, each described.
- Biomechanics: apply the named principle correctly (impulse, Newton's second law, etc.) to the specific movement and comparison.
- Fitness test selection: justify by **specificity** to the sport's energy system and movement demands (e.g. the Yo-Yo intermittent recovery test for repeated-sprint sports).
- Energy systems: use the data to analyse their interplay during activity *and recovery*. Steady-state predictions must be justified from the data.
- Progression via reduced rest: discuss suitability for the targeted energy system.
