# VCE Systems Engineering, Product Design and Technologies, Visual Communication Design

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports for each study. My own summary.

## Systems Engineering
**Exam:** 15 min reading + 1 h 30 min writing. Section A: 20 MC (20). Section B: short and extended answer (80). Includes diagrams, calculations, planning, design and evaluation in scenarios. Cross-study specifications (pp. 13–29) are examinable.
- **Calculations**: correct formula, substitution shown, **units** on the answer. Watch hidden steps: force = mass × g before work. Period → frequency for AC. Parallel resistor networks including tolerances (often in unconventional layouts).
- **Components**: identify by symbol (LED, phototransistor, polarised/electrolytic capacitor, bridge rectifier) **and** state their function in *this* circuit (e.g. smoothing a signal, AC → DC).
- **Energy**: name the type of energy lost or the process of loss. Explain energy conversions (mechanical → electrical in a dynamo/generator).
- **Justify** component or system choices with reasons tied to the scenario. Design circuits that are physically plausible (a dynamo attached to a rotating part).
- **Testing**: multimeter in **parallel** for voltage, in **series** for current. Evaluation criteria must link input to output (efficiency).
- **Discuss** = balanced positives and negatives (e.g. open-source software).
- **Production processes**: tie each process to the product, with specific safety measures.
- **Maintenance**: name the subsystems and give a specific maintenance procedure for each.
- Read the question. Answers that don't address it directly don't get full marks.

## Product Design and Technologies (Study Design from 2024)
**Exam:** 15 min reading + 1 h 30 min writing. 90 marks. Cross-study specifications (pp. 11–19) are examinable.
- **Ethical research methods**: pair a research method with *what makes it ethical* (consent, anonymity, etc.), not just a method or just an issue.
- **Data**: quantitative vs qualitative. Interpret graphs accurately. **Synthesise** = combine both graphs into one overarching conclusion (separate descriptions are capped). Justify improvements with evidence **from both graphs**. Only use features the data actually shows.
- **Annotations**: connect specific product features to **end-user needs** (study design "End user – factors that influence product design", p. 18). Generic feature lists earn nothing.
- **Analyse** two ethical considerations when asked, plus how they relate to the design.
- **Compare and critique** environmental impacts (e.g. repurposed vs virgin plastics): similarities *and* differences, positives *and* negatives, and why they matter. Use the stimulus.

## Visual Communication Design (Study Design from 2024)
**Exam:** 15 min reading + 1 h 30 min writing. Visual stimulus in a resource book. Section A (40) plus a further section on studied or presented design examples.
- **Terminology must be exact and from the study design**:
  - **Gestalt principles** ≠ design elements ≠ design principles.
  - **Methods vs media vs materials**: keep them distinct and apply each to visible features.
  - **Shape (2D) vs form (3D).**
  - The **five factors** that influence designers, named exactly.
- **Evaluate** = judge *how well* the methods, media and materials were applied, with specific visual evidence from the final solution, not "digital software was used".
- **Analyse** how one element *and* one principle together achieve an aesthetic quality.
- Influences: explain how a factor influenced **the designer's decisions**, not why the design was needed.
- **Conceptions of good design** (e.g. Dieter Rams' principles): link them directly to the design's **purpose** or **context**, not side by side. **Context** = the environment of use or display (exhibition, domestic, public, outdoor), not the presentation format.
- **Legal obligations** (IP, copyright, trademarks) explained with evidence from the example.
