# VCE Outdoor and Environmental Studies (revised Study Design, examined from 2024)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 90 marks.
- Section A (75–80): short and extended answer. Section B (10–15): one extended response, marked against published criteria.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Specific outdoor environments** are essential: name the place *within* the area (a named walk, beach or catchment), not just "Wilsons Promontory". Extended responses usually need **two environments** for full evidence marks.
- **Specific groups and examples**: a named Indigenous people and their environment, a named pre-1990 campaign, current federal parties or representatives with plausible positions.
- **Evaluate** = a clear judgement supported by evidence. **Compare** = at least two comparison points with explicit comparative language. **Analyse** = cause → effect chains (threat → environmental impact → impact on society).
- Use the stimulus (images, graphs) explicitly, and link images to the correct time periods.
- Move beyond description to discussion and analysis in extended responses.
- Exclusions apply (e.g. a threat "other than climate change").

## Content precision expected
- **Indigenous relationships before colonisation**: custodianship, cultural practices, land use, ecological knowledge.
- **Registered Aboriginal Parties**: the *process* to become one (formal criteria, application, approval pathway), not just their roles. Their influence on economic *and* conservation relationships.
- **Non-Indigenous relationships** across the study design's periods (including Post-Federation and the last decade).
- Methods for groups to **influence decision-makers**. Formal conflict resolution processes, evaluated for the specific conflict.
- **Sustainability pillars** (social, environmental, economic): identify them accurately and show *interdependence*, not just two separate descriptions.
- **Environmental health**: use study design observable characteristics (water quality, species biodiversity, etc.) and link the solution to the problems identified.
- **Land management**: contemporary terminology (cultural burning, co-management, cultural site protection). Know which actions only work on private land (Trust for Nature covenants, regenerative farming) and how adjacent action still helps.
- **Acts and conventions**: exact titles (don't modify them). Purpose, scope and key functions. Compare effectiveness in the given context (scope, reach, protections, relevance to the threats).
- National strategies linked to the data trend (e.g. emissions reduction → ocean heat).
- Recreation: participation changes over time, wellbeing links, environmental impacts.
