# VCE Biology (Study Design from 2022)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 h 30 min writing. 50% of study score. 120 marks.
- Section A: 40 MC (40). Section B: short and extended answer, often built around scenarios, experiments and articles (80).
- Key science skills, ethical understanding (study design ethical concepts) and the "social, economic, legal, political factors" skill are examinable.

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Restating the stem earns nothing** ("enzymes speed up reactions" when asked *how* enzymes catalyse; "reduces the spread" as a justification).
- **Use the context.** Pre-planned generic answers (booster vaccines, molecular homology between *species* when the question is about *populations*) don't score. Earlier stem information still applies to later parts.
- **Address every part**: both timeframes, both cells, both antibodies, both stages ("why it rose *and* why it plateaued").
- **Command terms**: "contrast" means differences only. "Identify the process" needs the process named.
- Spelling isn't penalised **unless** the misspelling is a different real term (stoma vs stroma).
- Dot points are fine. Use the mark allocation and the number of lines to judge depth.
- Each named factor/description pair is marked together: a description must match its factor.
- All boxes in a table must be right for some marks. Use a ruler to read graphs. Read axis labels (enzyme activity vs concentration).
- Write in blue/black pen, even for diagrams.

## Content traps assessors flagged
- **Photosynthesis/respiration**: know the inputs and outputs of each stage (light-dependent vs independent, glycolysis, Krebs, ETC) and their locations (cristae = ETC). Fermentation is anaerobic: "aerobic fermentation" is a contradiction. **Rubisco** binds O₂/CO₂ (not "carbon"). Avoid "denaturation" when the issue is Rubisco's affinity. C4 plants *fix* more CO₂, not "take up" more. C4 ≠ CAM. Bundle sheath and mesophyll are different cells.
- **Gene expression**: introns removed / exons spliced. Cap and poly-A tail on opposite ends. trp operon: the repressor binds the **operator**, not the promoter. Attenuation needs accurate detail.
- **Golgi**: give its specific role, not a generic account of exocytosis. Vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane, not the cell wall.
- **Proteome** = all proteins produced by an organism. Explain cell differentiation with reference to both named cells.
- **Biotech**: sgRNA function, Cas9, PAM site. "Transformed" here isn't bacterial transformation. Each gene goes into its own plasmid. Gel electrophoresis: DNA moves to the positive anode, separated by size (not charge per size).
- **Immunity**: humoral (extracellular) vs cell-mediated (intracellular). Don't confuse extracellular with "not yet in the body". Natural vs artificial and active vs passive are distinct, so don't mix them. Antibodies don't "kill" pathogens: they bind antigens on extracellular pathogens and trigger elimination. Memory cells are **not** passed from mother to baby. Antigenic drift ≠ the emergence of a new pathogen (that's shift). Herd immunity = fewer available hosts, so less transmission.
- **Evolution**: no Lamarckian wording ("adapted in order to survive"). Variation must already exist. Natural selection and drift change allele *frequencies*. **Mutation** creates new alleles. Bottleneck: explain the smaller gene pool and unrepresentative allele frequencies. Species evidence must show reproductive isolation or structural difference, not just size.
- **Human evolution**: features of footprints that show bipedalism. Interbreeding claims need specifics (which species, when). State the benefits of mtDNA, not just what it's used for.

## Science skills
- Limitations of a **model** ≠ limitations of the data or of the process being modelled.
- Validity: comment explicitly on whether results are valid, citing specific threats (limited sample demographic, more than one independent variable, subjective data, no random allocation).
- Qualitative vs quantitative. Primary vs secondary data.
- Interpret graph numbers with their meaning (3.5 = average likelihood out of 5, not 3.5%).
- **Ethics**: apply the study design's ethical concepts. Your justification must match the position you take. Choose realistic, ethical strategies (you wouldn't isolate a baby from its mother).
- Economic benefit claims must be specific and logical, not generic ("contributes to the economy").
