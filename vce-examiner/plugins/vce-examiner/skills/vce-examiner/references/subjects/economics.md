# VCE Economics (Study Design from 2023)

Sources: VCAA exam specifications, 2025 and 2024 external assessment reports. My own summary.

## Exam format
- 15 min reading + 2 hours writing. 50% of study score. 80 marks.
- Section A: 15 MC (15). Section B: short and extended answer, with stimulus (graphs, tables, quotes) (65).
- **Contemporary knowledge is examined**: current data on growth, unemployment, inflation and the terms of trade, the RBA's stance and the latest federal budget measures. Questions often say "over the past 2 years".

## Marking habits to replicate
- **Explain, don't assert.** Every economic relationship needs its mechanism (e.g. cash rate → transmission mechanism → AD → labour demand → employment).
- **Full sentences and paragraphs.** Arrows and symbol shorthand can't show complex relationships and are marked down.
- **Define precisely, once.** Learn study design definitions by heart. Don't redefine a goal throughout a question.
- **Use current data** with figures and dates when the question asks about recent performance.
- **Answer the exact focus**: characteristics, not consequences. The degree of responsiveness (PES), not the law of supply. Both "strong" *and* "sustainable". Both monetary *and* budgetary policy for "AD policies". The **context** given (e.g. global uncertainty) must be woven into every strength or weakness.
- Read the graph: state explicitly what happened to equilibrium price and quantity. Don't narrate adjustment steps that weren't asked for.
- *Discuss* = more than one impact or perspective (for and against).
- If you continue at the back of the booklet, signpost it clearly.

## Concept precision traps
- **Public goods**: non-rivalrous + **non-excludable** (it's hard to exclude *non-payers*, not "everyone can access them"). Public hospitals and education are *not* pure public goods. Free-riding is a consequence, not a characteristic.
- **Direct provision** = the government producing the good itself with taxpayer funds, not any intervention.
- **Opportunity cost** = the benefit forgone from the next-best alternative (not its price). Link it to relative scarcity.
- **Quantity supplied** (a point on the curve) ≠ **equilibrium quantity**.
- **PES** ≠ PED ≠ non-price supply factors. Explain *how* a factor changes responsiveness.
- **AD** = spending on Australian-produced goods and services, so imports are subtracted.
- **Terms of trade** = an index of export prices relative to import prices. It's **not** the value of exports vs imports and not net exports. Avoid using the exchange rate to explain TOT changes unless asked.
- **Full employment**: relative to NAIRU. Use participation and underemployment data. Explain the consequences for living standards.
- **Cost inflation**: shocks → higher production costs / lower AS → upward pressure on prices.
- **Monetary policy**: the **stance** is judged against the estimated **neutral** rate, not past cash rates. Explain the transmission mechanisms. Weaknesses: long **impact** lag (there's no implementation lag), less effective with high household debt, low confidence or the zero lower bound. "Can't target sectors" = blunt instrument (not accepted when excluded).
- **Budget**: variations from estimates come from **automatic stabilisers**. Don't confuse the budget outcome with the current account. Name a **specific current discretionary measure** from the latest budget.
- **Protection**: short-term vs long-term resource allocation. Tariffs on *Australian exports* abroad ≠ Australian tariffs on imports.
- **Skilled migration**: aggregate supply effects (labour force size and quality, productivity) vs short-term demand effects on CPI broadly, not just house prices.
- **Environmental policy**: market-based tools (ETS, carbon tax) and **intertemporal efficiency**, with short-run costs vs long-run gains.
